# 212207 — Manjhanpur block, Kaushambi. What is actually there, and what can be sold.

Every number below is either measured in this session or cited to its source.
Where something is an estimate it says so. Nothing here is from memory.

---

## 1. What 212207 is

India Post, checked today: **14 post offices, 13 of them Branch Post Offices
(villages), 1 Sub Post Office — Manjhanpur**, which is the district
headquarters of Kaushambi. Block: Manjhanpur. Tehsils in the district:
Manjhanpur, Sirathu, Chail.

District (Arth Ganga district report, IIM-L, citing Census 2011 and state GDDP
series):

  · Population **15,99,596**, **868 villages, 9 towns**, area 1,779 km²
  · Of all workers: **22.08% cultivators, 50.2% agricultural labourers,
    22.86% other workers**
  · **94.29% of landholdings are marginal or small**
  · Agriculture is "the backbone"; the district is "scantily industrialised"

in Prayagraj — *"you already pay for leads"* — has nobody to say it to, because
nobody here pays for leads.

block.** In rural UP an OSM "hospital" is usually a government PHC or CHC, not
a private buyer — but it is 17 more health facilities than this pincode looked
like it had, and it is checkable on the ground in an afternoon.

This is why the earlier plan does not transfer to this pincode. It is worth
knowing before a single day is spent on it.

## 3. Where the money actually is: banana

Kaushambi is called **"Mini Bhusawal"** and banana is the district's **ODOP
(One District One Product)**.

  · **2,550–4,000+ farmers**, on **6,000–10,000 hectares** — doubled from
    5,000 ha in 2020 (Hindustan Times, May 2024 and May 2026; district
    horticulture officer Avdhesh Mishra)
  · Concentrated in **Newada, Kaushambi and Chail blocks**
  · A **₹2 crore tissue culture lab** was built at Chilla Shahbazi in 2023
  · A **Kaushambi Banana Growers' Association** exists
  · Bananas already go to Delhi and Madhya Pradesh

Grower income, from a 100-farmer survey in the district (Pal & Maurya, Int. J.
Agriculture & Food Science, 2025): **50% of growers earn ₹10,000–30,000 per
month.** That is the top of the local income pyramid, and it is what a price
has to fit inside.

The same survey's price spread, per quintal:

| | ₹/quintal |
|---|---|
| Farmer's selling price | 800 |
| Marketing cost | 150 |
| Wholesaler margin | 200 |
| Retailer margin | 250 |
| **Consumer pays** | **1,400** |

The farmer keeps 57.1%. The remaining ₹600 is where the local traders,
commission agents and wholesalers live — and those are the people in this
belt with cash.

## 4. The number one problem, ranked by the farmers themselves

Garrett ranking, same 100-farmer survey:

| Rank | Constraint | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Lack of processing units** | 72.3 |
| II | Price fluctuation | 65.1 |
| III | Post-harvest losses | 59.2 |
| IV | Inadequate storage | 52.8 |
| V | Poor market access | 46.9 |

And in plain words from a grower in Tilgodi village, Newada block: *"the lack
of food processing units means that a good harvest brings down the prices,
greatly affecting the farmers as excess fruits would rot unless sold
immediately."*

Six years after banana was made the district's ODOP, **the processing unit
still has not been built.** That gap is the single largest economic fact in
this pincode.

## 5. The government already pays somebody to fix this. That is the opening.

**PMFME** — Pradhan Mantri Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises.
Verified against the MoFPI scheme guidelines and the official DRP Manual.

What a beneficiary gets:

  · **35% credit-linked capital subsidy, ceiling ₹10 lakh per individual unit**
  · Beneficiary puts in 10%, the bank lends the rest
  · **₹3 crore ceiling** for FPO / SHG / cooperative group projects
  · SHG members: **₹40,000 seed capital per member**
  · New units are supported **only for the ODOP product** — in Kaushambi that
    is banana, which is exactly what is grown here

### The hard rule that decides how this is sold

Straight from the official FAQ:

> *"Is any processing fees or application fees will be charged? — No, it's a
> centrally sponsored scheme, you need not to pay any fees for filling
> application or any handholding."*

**So a farmer cannot be charged for PMFME help. Anyone charging one is
breaking the scheme.** That closes the obvious idea before it costs anything.

### What the scheme pays instead

**Route A — become a District Resource Person (DRP).**
The DRP Manual states who can be appointed: *"Any suitable person(s) (like
retired Government/Bank Officials, Insurance agents, Bank Mitras, **Consultancy
firms, Individual professionals**, etc.) ... could be appointed as DRP by the
State Nodal Agency (SNA)."*

Zesst Now Services Private Limited is a consultancy firm and is eligible on the
face of the manual.

Payment, verbatim: **₹20,000 per bank loan sanctioned** — ₹10,000 on uploading
the sanction letter, ₹10,000 on facilitating FSSAI + Udyam + GST registration.
Paid by the State Nodal Agency, not by the farmer.

**Route B — DPR preparation for groups.**
*"Assistance of Rs. 50,000/- per case would be provided to FPOs/SHGs/
Cooperatives for preparation of DPR"* — and critically: *"Engaging District
Resource Person (DRP) for this component is not mandatory. The applicant
organization may engage **any professional / agencies having experience in
preparation of DPR**."*

**This is the one place a fee can be charged directly, it is ₹50,000 per case,
and the scheme funds it.** The Kaushambi Banana Growers' Association is exactly
the kind of body this clause is written for.

FPO eligibility for group capital: minimum ₹1 crore turnover, 3 years'
experience with the product, project cost not larger than current turnover.

## 6. What this is worth per month — estimate, clearly labelled

These are arithmetic on the scheme's own published rates, not observed
earnings. Nothing here has been earned yet.

**Route A, DRP:**

| Sanctions per month | Monthly |
|---|---|
| 2 | ₹40,000 |
| 5 | ₹1,00,000 |
| 10 | ₹2,00,000 |

**Route B, group DPR:** ₹50,000 per case. Two a month is ₹1,00,000.

**The honest brake on both:** DRP money arrives **after the bank sanctions the
loan**, and only half of it then — the rest after FSSAI, Udyam and GST are
done. From first meeting to first rupee is realistically **2–4 months**. This
is not this week's cash. It is a pipeline that, once running, pays every month
without new marketing spend, because the government is the one advertising.

## 7. The upsell, and why it is the better half

Look at what the DRP is required to deliver for that ₹20,000: **FSSAI licence,
Udyam registration, GST registration.**

Every single unit that gets a PMFME loan therefore becomes a **GST-registered
food business** — and a GST-registered business has to file **every month, for
as long as it trades.**

That is the annuity. The subsidy work is paid acquisition — paid by the State.
The filing work is the recurring revenue.

| Service | When | Typical |
|---|---|---|
| DRP fee | per sanction | ₹20,000 (from SNA) |
| Group DPR | per case | ₹50,000 (scheme-funded) |
| GST monthly filing | every month, forever | ₹500–1,500/unit |
| FSSAI renewal | yearly | one-off each |
| Udyam / licence updates | as needed | one-off each |

30 units on monthly filing at ₹1,000 is **₹30,000/month recurring**, on top of
the per-case fees — and those 30 clients cost nothing to acquire, because the
scheme delivered them.

Zesst Now already runs a GST product, so the filing side is not a new capability
to build. *(No change to that product is proposed or implied here.)*

## 8. What has to be true before any of this is real

Stated plainly, because the whole thing rests on it:

1. **The SNA has to appoint him as a DRP.** The manual says a consultancy firm
   is eligible; it does not say appointment is automatic. UPDIC / the UP state
   nodal agency decides. **This is the gate, and it is unverified.**
2. **A bank has to sanction the loan.** No sanction, no fee. The DRP carries
   the work and the wait.
3. **The DPR has to survive scrutiny** — the portal rejects non-viable
   financials outright: DSCR must be above 1.5, positive break-even, roughly 8%
   profit, payback between 5 and 7 years. A weak DPR fails at the portal, not
   at the bank.

## 9. What I would do first, in order

1. **Find out who the Kaushambi DRP is today, and whether the district has a
   vacancy.** One phone call to the District Industries Centre, Manjhanpur, or
   the district horticulture office. This is a 10-minute call that decides
   whether any of this exists.
2. **Go to the Kaushambi Banana Growers' Association.** They are the named
   group, the ODOP is their crop, and Route B (₹50,000 DPR, scheme-funded)
   needs exactly one such body to say yes.
3. **Ask the district horticulture officer** — Avdhesh Mishra is named in the
   press as the serving officer — how many PMFME applications the district has
   moved and where they stall. That answer tells you whether the pipeline is
   empty because there is no demand, or because there is nobody doing the
   paperwork.

None of those three needs a rupee or a website. All three are phone calls or a
visit, and all three are things I cannot do from here.

## The one-line version

**In 212207 nobody buys websites, because nobody in this belt is online at
all — measured, not assumed. What exists is banana, a district-wide failure to
process it, and a central scheme that pays a consultancy ₹20,000 per case and
₹50,000 per group DPR to fix precisely that. The money is not from the farmer;
it is from the scheme. And every unit it creates needs GST filed every month
afterwards — which is where the recurring income is.**

The gate is a DRP appointment from the state nodal agency. Until that is
checked, everything above is a plan and not a business.
