# The new-business pitch

Same brief as the advocate and CA messages: speak as the customer, not the
seller. Name their weakness, name their profit, say nothing about us, then one
CTA. It must land as *"I needed this and today it arrived"*, never as
*"somebody is selling me something."*

But this buyer is **not** the advocate, and copying that message here would
fail. The difference is worth being precise about, because the whole pitch
turns on it.

---

## Why this buyer is different

An advocate on the Allahabad roll has practised for twenty years and has never
needed a website. He has to be shown that a second stream of clients exists at
all. The message has to create the problem in his mind.

A business founded in 2024 or 2025 **already knows.** It registered a LinkedIn
page. Most of them bought a domain. They have already decided the internet
matters — they just ran out of time, money or a person who could do it. Nobody
here has to be convinced that a website is worth having.

So the advocate pitch's core move — *"search your own name, see what's
missing"* — is wasted on them. They know what's missing. They look at it every
day.

**What this buyer needs is not persuasion. It is permission to stop putting it
off.** That is a completely different message: shorter, more concrete, and it
must reference the specific thing wrong with THEIR site, or it is just another
agency mail.

---

## The one thing that makes this list work

Every row carries a `problem` code that was **measured on their live site**,
not guessed. That code is the first line of the message, and it is the only
reason this is not spam:

| code | what was measured | the opening line writes itself |
|---|---|---|
| `NO-SITE` | LinkedIn lists no domain at all | they are invisible outside LinkedIn |
| `DEAD` | the domain does not answer | **they are probably paying for it** |
| `PARKED` | answers with a "coming soon" holding page | they started and stopped |
| `THIN` | under ~8 KB of markup — one screen | it is a placeholder, not a site |
| `BUILDER` | Wix / GoDaddy / Google Sites template | customers can tell |
| `NO-HTTPS` | http only | the browser says **"Not secure"** |
| `OK` | a real site | do not sell a website — sell the app |

`DEAD` is the best row in the file and it deserves its own note. A domain that
does not resolve is almost always still being paid for annually. That is a
person who is spending money on nothing, every year, and has not noticed. You
are not selling him something new. You are telling him about a leak.

## E-MAIL — for `DEAD`, `PARKED`, `THIN`, `NO-HTTPS`

Subject lines. No offer, no company name, nothing that reads as a campaign:

  · `<domain> — ये खुल नहीं रहा`
  · `आपकी site पर एक बात`
  · `<Company> — 30 सेकंड की बात`

Body:

```
नमस्ते,

<domain> अभी खोल कर देखिए अपने फ़ोन पर।

मैंने देखा तो <वो जो असल में हुआ — नहीं खुली / "coming soon" लिखा
आया / browser ने "Not secure" दिखाया>।

आपका ग्राहक भी यही देखता है। और वो दोबारा कोशिश नहीं करता — वो
अगला नाम खोल लेता है।

<अगर DEAD हो:>
और एक बात — ये domain शायद अब भी हर साल renew हो रहा है। यानी
जिस चीज़ के पैसे जा रहे हैं, वो चल ही नहीं रही।

हिसाब आपका है, मेरा नहीं: आपका एक ग्राहक आपके लिए कितने का है?
महीने में दो भी इस वजह से लौट गए तो साल भर में कितना बना?

मैं आपके नाम पर एक पन्ना बनाकर दिखा सकता हूँ — बिना किसी शुल्क
के, बिना किसी वादे के। पसंद आए तो आगे बात, न आए तो बात ख़त्म।

सिर्फ़ "हाँ" लिख दीजिए।

— सोनू शर्मा
Zesst Now Services Private Limited
WhatsApp +91 77538 98481
```

**Why it is built this way.** The first line is an instruction, so whatever he
finds, HE found it. The second line is a measurement, not an adjective — and it
has to be the real one from the `problem` column, which is the entire reason
that column exists. The money paragraph refuses to name a number and hands him
his own arithmetic. The ask is one word with the exit written next to it.

**Nothing about us until he asks.** No service list, no years of experience, no
"we are a leading…". We are a person who noticed something.

## WhatsApp — the same thing, short

New businesses answer WhatsApp and ignore e-mail. This is the whole message:

```
नमस्ते 🙏

<domain> अभी खोलकर देखिए — <नहीं खुल रही / "coming soon" आ रहा है>।

आपका ग्राहक भी यही देखता है, और वो अगला नाम खोल लेता है।

एक पन्ना बनाकर दिखा दूँ? मुफ़्त — देख लीजिए, पसंद आए तो आगे।
```

---

## `OK` rows — do not send either message

These have a working, decent website. Telling them their site is bad is a lie
they can check in one click, and it burns the row permanently.

For them the pitch is the **app**, and the list already carries the evidence:
`has_app` says whether any Play Store or App Store link appears anywhere on
their site.

```
नमस्ते,

<domain> देखी — काम की बनी है, सच में।

एक चीज़ नहीं मिली: कोई app नहीं है।

आपका ग्राहक साल में एक बार आपकी website खोलता है। App फ़ोन में
रहता है — वो हर दिन दिखता है। यही पूरा फ़र्क़ है।

आपके जैसे <industry> के काम में ये कैसा दिखेगा, एक नमूना बनाकर
भेज दूँ? मुफ़्त।

"हाँ" लिख दीजिए।
```

---

## When he replies "हाँ"

**Do not send a quote.** A quote turns a conversation back into a sale.

Build the page. One page, his business name, what he sells, his address, his
phone. Two hours. Send the link with one line:

```
ये देख लीजिए — <link>
कुछ बदलना हो तो बता दीजिए।
```

Now something with his own name on it exists. The money conversation happens
after that, from a completely different position: he is not deciding whether to
buy, he is deciding whether to keep.

**Then the price**, one number, never a range:

> ₹25,000 — पूरी website, 1 साल hosting, Google पर listing, WhatsApp button.
> ₹6,250 अभी, बाक़ी काम पूरा होने पर.

App work is a different conversation and a different number — that is the
₹3.5L–₹10L product band in `pricing.py`, not this.

---

## Order of work

The file is already sorted for this. Work it top to bottom:

1. **`NO-SITE`** — nothing to lose, nothing to defend.
2. **`DEAD`** — paying for something broken. The strongest message in the set.
3. **`PARKED`** — they started and stopped. The intent is proven.
4. **`THIN`** — a placeholder someone already settled for.
5. **`BUILDER` / `NO-HTTPS`** — a real site with a real flaw; softer.
6. **`OK`** — app pitch only, and only where `has_app` is `no`.

---

## The two rules that keep this list alive

**Every row goes through `seen.py`.** These are real businesses and one
relevant message with an easy way out is defensible; a second and a third are
not. Follow up at 3 days and 7 days, once each, then close the row.

**Never send a `problem` code you have not re-checked that morning.** A site
that was `DEAD` in September may be live in October, and an opening line that
is factually wrong destroys the only advantage this list has — that everything
in it was measured.
