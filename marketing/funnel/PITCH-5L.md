# The ₹5–10 lakh pitch — founder track

Written 15 September 2026, replacing the white-label agency pitch for this
buyer. The agency pitch sells HOURS to a reseller. This sells an OUTCOME to
the person whose money it is. They are not the same email and must never be
mixed.

---

## Before a single email goes out — the thing that breaks everything else

`pricing.py` caps at ₹2.5L (Platform ₹1–1.5L, App both ₹1.5–2.5L). The blog
post published 14 Sep and `/partners` both state those bands publicly.

A founder who is about to spend ₹6L **will** look you up. When your own site
says ₹1.5L, the ₹6L quote is finished before the call. You will have
negotiated against yourself using your own website.

**Either the price book gets a product-build tier above ₹2.5L, or the ask
stays under ₹2.5L.** There is no third option, and shipping the emails before
fixing this wastes the list.

---

## The five sales moves this pitch is built on

Named so they can be reused, not just copied.

**1 · Observation open, never an apology.**
Open with one specific, checkable fact about THEM. Not "your team is small,
which usually means" — that is a guess wearing a fact's clothes and a founder
spots it in one second. Use something measured: their site is a Framer
template, their domain serves a placeholder, they have no app, their page has
no meta description. We measured all of it for 100 companies. That opening
line is the entire reason the email gets read.

**2 · Insight, not information.**
They already know their site is on Webflow. What they have not connected is
the consequence. "Webflow is fine until you need a login" is information.
"Your pricing page asks people to book a demo because the product cannot take
a payment yet — that is a Webflow ceiling, not a strategy choice" is an
insight. Insight earns the reply; information earns a delete.

**3 · Anchor, then drop.**
Never name your price first and never name it naked. Name what a Bangalore
agency charges for the same build, then name yours. ₹6L after ₹14L reads as
value. ₹6L on its own reads as expensive. Both are the same number.

**4 · Kill the risk out loud — this is the one that closes.**
The real objection is never price. It is: *"who is this person, and what
happens to my ₹6L if he disappears?"* They will not say it. Answer it anyway,
in writing, before they ask:

  · milestone payments — they pay for what is already delivered
  · the code sits in THEIR GitHub from day one, not ours
  · a small paid first milestone, so the first cheque is not the big one
  · they can stop after any milestone and keep everything built so far

Every one of those costs us nothing and removes the only thing standing
between a yes and a maybe. An unknown vendor from a small town who removes the
risk beats a known agency that does not.

**5 · One specific ask.**
"Let me know if interested" is not an ask. "Twenty minutes on Thursday — I
will come with a build plan for the login and payments piece, and if it is not
useful you have lost twenty minutes" is an ask. Name the length, name the
agenda, name what they lose if it is bad.

## THE EMAIL

Subject lines — no price, no pitch, just the observation. Pick per lead:

  · `<Company>'s site vs what <Company> actually does`
  · `the login you cannot build on Webflow`
  · `<company>.com is serving a placeholder`

Body. Under 150 words. Everything in <> gets replaced per lead — an email
where one of these is left generic is worse than not sending it.

```
Hi <FirstName>,

<ONE MEASURED FACT. Examples:>
<· decisionx.ai is on Webflow — good design, but it cannot hold a login.>
<· plutussolutions.in is serving a placeholder page.>
<· thyne.ai does not load at all right now.>

<THE CONSEQUENCE, in their language:>
You are selling <what they actually sell, from their own description> to
<their buyer>. That buyer wants to <sign in / pay / see their data>, and
right now the site cannot let them. Everything after the demo call is
manual.

I build the thing the site cannot: <the specific piece — auth, payments,
dashboard, admin, the Android app>. A Bangalore agency quotes ₹12–15L and
four months for this. I do it in milestones, starting at ₹<X>L.

How the money works, so you are not taking a risk on a stranger:
you pay per milestone, after it ships. The repository is yours from
day one. Stop after any milestone and you keep everything built.

Twenty minutes this week? I will bring a build plan for <the specific
piece>, free either way.

<Name>
Zesst Now Services Pvt Ltd · www.cognitivecapitalsuite.com
```

**Why it is short.** A founder reads on a phone between meetings. Every
sentence that is not about them is a reason to stop reading. There is no
company introduction, no "we are a passionate team", no service list. They
do not care yet, and they never will unless the first two lines land.

## THE LINKEDIN DM — under 300 characters

LinkedIn kills long DMs. This is the whole message:

```
<FirstName> — <company>.com is on Webflow. Fine for the site, but it cannot
hold the login your customers need. I build that piece, milestone-paid, your
repo. Worth 20 minutes?
```

Nothing else. No link, no rate, no pitch deck. The reply IS the goal.

## WHEN THEY REPLY — the call, and where these calls are lost

They will not say yes on the call. They will decide whether to trust you.

**First four minutes: do not pitch. Ask.**
  · What are you building right now, and what is in the way?
  · Who is doing the development today?
  · If this existed next month, what changes for the business?

Let them talk. The person who talks more feels better about the call, and
their words are the words that go in your proposal.

**Then say back what you heard,** before proposing anything. "So the product
works, but every new customer is onboarded by hand and that is capping you at
about twenty a month." If that lands, you have already won — almost nobody
who cold-emails them does this.

**Only then, the scope, as milestones with money attached:**
```
  M1  Auth + accounts                 ₹1.2L    2 weeks
  M2  Payments + subscription         ₹1.5L    2 weeks
  M3  Customer dashboard              ₹2.0L    3 weeks
  M4  Admin + reporting               ₹1.3L    2 weeks
                                      ─────
                                      ₹6.0L    9 weeks
```
Milestones do three things at once: the ₹6L stops being one frightening
number, they can start small, and every payment has something delivered
behind it.

**Close on the smallest step, never the whole thing:**
"Start with M1. ₹1.2L, two weeks. If you do not like the work, you stop and
you keep the code." Almost nobody says no to that, and nobody who says yes to
M1 goes shopping again for M2.

**The three objections, and the honest answer to each:**

*"You are in Prayagraj, we are in Bangalore."* — True, and the work is
remote either way. Your repo, daily pushes, a call whenever you want. Judge
M1 on the code, and if it is not good you have lost two weeks and ₹1.2L.

*"Send a proposal."* — Usually a polite no. Push once: "I will, tonight.
Before I do — is the budget for this quarter or next?" Their answer tells
you whether the proposal is worth writing.

*"Too expensive."* — Never drop the price. Drop the scope. "Then let us cut
M3 and M4 for now — M1 and M2 get you paying customers, ₹2.7L." Cutting
price teaches them the first number was fake. Cutting scope teaches them the
price is real.

## What this pitch does NOT do, and the honest version of the timeline

It does not close today. An email sent this morning is read tonight or
tomorrow; a founder who replies still wants a call, and ₹5–10L is signed
after that call, not in it. Realistically: reply in 1–3 days, call in 3–7,
M1 signed in 1–3 weeks. Anyone who tells you otherwise about a ₹6L project
from a cold start is selling you something.

What CAN happen today is the part that decides all of it — the emails go out
sharp instead of generic, the price book stops undercutting the ask, and the
first call gets booked.
