Two days is enough for a real website. It is not enough for every website, and
the difference is worth being honest about.

## What makes 48 hours possible

**The scope is fixed before the clock starts.** We agree exactly what pages
exist and what each says. Adding a page halfway is what turns two days into two
weeks — not the building.

**The content exists.** This is the real constraint. Design and code are fast;
waiting for someone to write their own About page is not. We can write it from a
conversation, but that conversation has to happen at the start.

**One team, no handoffs.** Nobody is waiting for a designer in another city to
send a file to a developer who has not read the brief.

**The infrastructure is solved already.** Hosting, deployment, SSL, analytics,
forms, performance budgets — we are not deciding those per project. That is
weeks of decisions that simply do not recur.

## What actually happens

**Hours 0–4 — brief and structure.** What the business does, who it is for, what
a visitor should do. Then the page list and what each page has to achieve. This
is the only part where being slow is worth it; everything downstream inherits
the mistakes made here.

**Hours 4–16 — design and copy.** Real pages with real words, not a mood board.
You see the actual thing, on a staging link, from the first day.

**Hours 16–36 — build.** Structure, responsive behaviour, motion, forms. Built
for speed and search from the start, because retrofitting either is expensive.

**Hours 36–44 — the unglamorous half.** Metadata, Open Graph, structured data,
sitemap, analytics, form testing on real devices, keyboard and screen reader
checks, Core Web Vitals. This is what separates a site that ranks from a site
that merely exists.

**Hours 44–48 — review and launch.** Your round of changes, domain pointed, SSL
live, handover.

## When 48 hours is the wrong promise

We say so rather than take the work:

- **Web applications.** Logins, dashboards, payments, user data. These are
  scoped and time-locked separately, and the timeline is honest.
- **Ecommerce with real catalogue depth.** A few products, fine. Hundreds of
  SKUs with variants and inventory, no.
- **Content that does not exist yet.** Fifty pages of legal or medical copy
  cannot be written responsibly in two days.
- **Multi-stakeholder approval.** If four people must agree on the headline, the
  constraint is the meetings, not us.
