Most SEO advice is written for businesses selling nationally online. If you sell
in one district, most of it does not apply to you and some of it wastes money.

Here is what actually matters, roughly in order.

## 1. Your Google Business Profile

For a local business this outranks everything else you could do. It is what
populates the map pack, and the map pack sits above organic results.

Fill it in completely — every field. Correct category, exact service area,
accurate hours including holidays, phone number, and real photographs of the
actual premises. Post updates. Answer questions.

**Reviews matter more than almost anything else you control.** Ask every
satisfied customer, make it easy, and reply to all of them including the
critical ones. A thoughtful reply to a bad review reads better to a prospect
than the absence of bad reviews.

## 2. Consistent name, address and phone

Your business name, address and phone number must be **identical** everywhere
they appear — your site, your profile, directories, your invoices. Not
approximately identical. "Shop 4" and "Shop No. 4" are different strings, and
inconsistency dilutes the signal that all these listings are the same business.

Pick one canonical format and use it everywhere.

## 3. Pages that answer the actual question

A single page listing every service ranks for nothing. Separate pages, each
answering one specific question, rank for that question.

"GST registration in Kaushambi" is a page. "Home loan documents required" is a
page. Write them properly — what the person needs, what it costs, how long it
takes, what to bring.

This is also what gets you quoted by AI answer engines, which increasingly sit
between a search and a click. They quote specific, well-structured answers, not
service menus.

## 4. Technical basics

None of this is glamorous and all of it is load-bearing.

- **Fast on a phone**, on an ordinary connection. Most local searches are mobile.
- **Structured data** — LocalBusiness markup with address, hours and geography
  so search engines read your details as facts rather than as text.
- **A real sitemap**, submitted.
- **One canonical URL** per page.
- **Genuinely accessible**: readable contrast, sensible headings, working
  keyboard navigation. It correlates with what ranks and it is the right thing
  regardless.

## 5. Local relevance signals

Mentions from local sources — a trade association, a local news item, a supplier
directory. These are earned rather than bought, and they are worth more than
purchased links, which mostly carry risk.

## What to ignore

Anyone guaranteeing a #1 position. Nobody controls the ranking, and a guarantee
is a sign of either ignorance or a plan to game something that will later be
penalised. Bulk directory submissions. Keyword stuffing. Buying links.
