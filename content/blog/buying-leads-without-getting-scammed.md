The lead market works on an asymmetry: the seller knows how old the data is and
the buyer does not. Everything below is about closing that gap before you pay.

## Test before you buy at scale

Never buy a large batch first. Buy the smallest lot the supplier will sell and
call every one of them within 24 hours.

You are checking four things:

**Are they real?** Wrong numbers, dead numbers, people who have never heard of
the category.

**Are they fresh?** "I enquired about that six months ago" means the data has
been sitting, and probably been sold before.

**Are they exclusive?** "Three people have already called me about this" means
you bought the same list as your competitors. Exclusivity should be in writing.

**Did they actually enquire?** This is the one that matters most, and the one
suppliers are vaguest about. Someone who filled a form asking about home loans is
a lead. Someone whose number was scraped is not — they are a complaint waiting
to happen.

## Price against your own numbers

Ignore what the supplier says a lead is worth. Work it out:

- What is a closed customer worth to you in gross profit?
- What share of leads in this category do you actually close?
- Multiply. That is what a lead is worth to you.

Pay meaningfully less than that. If the seller's price is close to your ceiling,
there is no business in it even if every lead is genuine.

Market rates move with category, season and geography — insurance in December is
not insurance in June. Any supplier quoting one flat rate across everything is
not pricing on data.

## The consent question

This decides whether you should be in the transaction at all.

Ask the supplier plainly: where did the consent come from, and can you show it?
A serious supplier can describe the source — a campaign, a comparison site, a
form — and produce evidence. One who cannot is selling scraped data, and the
exposure transfers to you the moment you dial.

India's data protection regime and telecom rules on unsolicited commercial
communication both bite here. The reputational cost of being the company that
cold-calls people who never asked is separate and larger.

## Selling leads

The same rules run in reverse. Sell fresh, sell exclusive, be able to evidence
consent, and price against what the buyer can realistically earn. A supplier who
does those four things keeps buyers for years; one who does not gets one sale per
customer.
